A normal distribution is, well, a type of distribution.
Its a way for a collection of numbers to be ... distributed?
I'm not sure how else to describe that.

It forms a bell curve.
The mean and median are equal.
So, 50% of values are above the average, and 50% are below.

We measure regions of the curve by "standard deviations" from the mean.

* ~68% of datapoints are within +/- 1 standard deviation of the center.
* ~95% are within 2 standard deviations.
* ~99.7% are within 3, and so forth.

In a default normal distribution,
the mean is 0 and the standard deviation is 1.

---

To shift the mean of a distribution,
you would add the same value to each datapoint.
If you add 1 to a ND with a mean of 0, think about it, the new mean is 1.

To shift the standard deviation,
you would multiply each datapoint by the same value.
If you multiply by 5 for a ND with a standard deviation of 1,
think about it, the new standard deviation is 5.

---

The CDF, Cumulative Distribution Function,
can be used to find the approximate percentage of the distribution
that falls within a given standard deviation.
