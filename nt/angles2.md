
An angle exists for any two lines which converge at a point.
It is a description of the direction/slope of one line relative to the other.






Theres really 2 main ways to represent an angle.

* Start from an arbitrary point on a circle,
  and measure the angle by the distance you travel around the circle.
  This is how degrees and raidans work, for instance.
* Represent the angle by its lines.
  Angles in this format are often more versatile, but less tidy.


With the first method, you have to pick a circle.
Degrees use a circle with a circumference of 360,
while radians use the unit-circle (aith a circumference of `2pi`).

Whatever you end up with:
* `circumference/2` is turning directly around
* `circumference/4` is a right angle
* `circumference/8` has a slope of 1





terminal vs initial side

srart with a circle centered around a point.
For the terminal,
it starts at the center and passes through the circle.
From the point at which it passes through the circle,
measure the distance along the curve
to the point the base-side passes through the circle.
That's the basis for measuring your angle.

Ofc, this can differ depending on the size of the circle
that your system uses.
Degrees use a circle with a circumference of 360,
and radians use the unit-circle
(with a circumference of 2pi).

This angle-representation can seem cumbersome in abstract or on paper,
but it makes a lot of sense when considering how
one would actually, physically, mechanically, measure an angle
in the real world with tools or machinery.
