	differentials are an infinitesimal change in a variable.
	They are denoted as: `dx`, meaning an infinitesimal change in `x`.
	This is similar to the notation `DELTAx`, as a change in `x`.

	how to explain the idea without calculus?

	also its relation to derivatives:

		dy = (dy/dx)dx
		where (dy/dx) is the derivative of `y`

	... since its a ratio of infinitesimal changes.
	The notation here is a bit awkward since
	we use differential-like symbols to denote derivatives.
	Its basically just representing the idea:

		dy(x)/dx = lim[DELTAx->0] DELTAy(x)/DELTAx


	With integrals we write:

		SLONG f(x)dx

	... since its the 'height' times an infinitely-thin width
